Sense of Self Expression

My sense of self-expression is very intense.

I see myself in everything

in the way a film unspools like my thoughts,

in a song that breathes in sync with my pulse,

in a book that spills the words

I thought only I could feel.

I express myself through anything I can,

through anything that fits within

the highest tier of my own hierarchy.

And so, I collect them

a thousand films, a thousand books, a thousand songs

I call myself their epitome.

But I was never in the room when they were made.

No lyric carries my ink,

no frame was bent by my hands.

I have no part in their genesis,

yet I wear them like a second skin.

This leaves me with a sense of originality

that is not mine to claim,

a gnawing knowledge that I am, at my core,

a composition of everything before me.

Everything I feel,

everyone I resemble,

everywhere I exist,

none of it is truly mine.

And so, guilt lingers when they call me creative.

Because everything I am has been someone before,

and every way I am myself

is merely the ghost of an influence.

But if nothing is mine,

then nothing is anyone’s.

And if everything has been before,

then I, too, have been before.

Perhaps I am not original,

but perhaps originality is not real.

But then again,

my sense of self-expression is very intense.
