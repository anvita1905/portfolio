Two Bombs, One Dilemma

They waited in the womb of a fighter jet, suspended in the machinery of precision. Two bombs, identical in design, but carrying the burden of two very different convictions.

The first bomb spoke with unwavering pride, as his casing lustred in the sterile red light. “My path is towards a school. A place where, in time, adversaries might be nurtured. My descent is not cruelty but necessity. Nations survive by pre-empting threats, by ensuring that their enemies never reach maturity. I will not apologise for serving my Republic. I am loyalty incarnated, an indispensable safeguard of order.”

The second bomb, bound for a hospital, was less certain. His voice carried the tremor of conscience. “Safeguard? You call the incineration of classrooms security? You call the destruction of incubators a strategy? I was briefed that my target harbours no weapons, only wounds. Patients recovering from shellfire, children tethered to ventilators, women in labour. Geneva forbids this. Humanity forbids this. Tell me, how does a democracy remain legitimate when it violates the very laws it claims to defend?”

The school-bomb bristled. “You speak like a philosopher, not a weapon. International law is noble on paper, but irrelevant in war zones. Collateral damage is not murder, it is arithmetic. Our nation does not unleash us for malice, but for deterrence. The message I deliver is simple: no sanctuary exists for those who oppose us. That clarity, however brutal, preserves more lives in the long arc of history than your hesitation ever could.”

The hospital-bomb quivered. “Arithmetic? You reduce a newborn’s final breath to a number on a ledger? You confuse deterrence with devastation. Look at Aleppo, at Mariupol, each time the world witnesses schools levelled, and hospitals razed, hatred does not diminish; it multiplies. The Security Council convenes endlessly on this very principle: that civilians must not be made pawns of war. To ignore this is not strength, it is strategic amnesia. And it will come back, again and again, in cycles of vengeance.”

The first bomb’s steel rang with certainty. “History is written by victors, not mourners. Our republic cannot hesitate because sentiment is inconvenient. When the council dithers, when the law collapses under vetoes, power alone ensures survival. I fall proudly, knowing that my explosion will sound of sovereignty.”

The second bomb’s voice lowered, nearly drowned by the roaring engines. “Then I will fall as an indictment. Not against my nation alone, but against a system that has allowed civilians to become the currency in geopolitical bargaining. We were built to serve strategy, but perhaps our detonation will remind the world of what unilateralism and strategy has forgotten: that a war waged without humanity is not victory, it is ruin.”

Above them, the fighter jet’s hatch yawned open as the air was split by silence. Two bombs plunged, both to suffer the same fate, one swathed in patriotism, the other in objection. Both confident in their own convictions, never with words, always with fire.
