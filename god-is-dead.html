God Is Dead

God is dead. Not the divine, not the force that pulses under skin and sky, but the name we dragged through centuries of blood. God, the idea, was killed by those who tried to own Him. Or Her. Or It.

Killed by men who needed control. Rewritten by hands that turned divinity into a weapon, and made entire generations fear the sky. That God, rigid, male, vengeful, was never real. He was a tool. A weapon used to discipline the masses, elevate the few, and crush the wild chaos of existence into a system that could be taxed, feared, and obeyed. We buried God beneath temples and churches and mosques, wrapped in scriptures written by men who mistook fear for faith and control for salvation. Religion didn’t save us. It chained us. Religion wasn’t born from truth. It was born from power. It disguised control as virtue, made obedience sacred, and guilt a currency. It demanded women shrink, men harden, and entire civilizations bleed in the name of salvation. It was never about connection. It was about hierarchy.

But still, something watches.

It's not hell or heaven or sin. You feel it. When your skin prickles for no reason. When you stare too long into the dark and feel the dark stare back. Something is there. Something ancient. Indifferent. Infinite. And you know it. You feel it in the static between your thoughts, in the peculiarity of coincidences. It's not interested in morality or dogma. It just is. Its presence without pretense. Above. Around. Beyond.

Ants don't know we're watching them. Fish don't know the sky exists. We are not the peak of knowing. We are blind in ways we'll never grasp. Think about this: we live in the third dimension. Our biology traps us in a space where depth, width, and height define reality. But that's just one layer. Physics, real, cutting-edge, peer-reviewed physics, tells us there are higher dimensions. A fourth or fifth. Realities beyond human perception. If we can't perceive beyond three, who's to say higher beings don't exist? Beings evolved in dimensions where our laws of time, space, and logic collapse?

We call them gods. Spirits. Aliens. We feel them in myth and mathematics. They are not above us because they are moral; they are above us because they exist outside the architecture of our knowing. Religion tried to cage it, brand it, and sell it. But the real God? The real God doesn’t care what you wear or who you love. The real God doesn’t want your fear.

So no, God isn't dead. The institution is. The costume is. What remains is purer. Stranger. Something that doesn't need a name but watches all the same.

And if you’re quiet enough, you can feel it watching now.
